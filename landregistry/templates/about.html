Case study

About this land registry

Ubutaka is a working prototype for a blockchain-based land registration and ownership verification system, framed as a case study for the Ministry of Local Government (MINALOC) in Rwanda.

The problem it answers

Paper files and disconnected databases make it hard to see who changed a land record, and when. A buyer, a family, or a sector office can be left with two stories about the same parcel.

This system keeps one current register in MySQL and an append-only hash chain beside it. A correction does not erase the earlier entry. It adds a new block that points at the previous one.

What the ledger stores

Each registration, legal update, and ownership verification becomes a block. The block holds the action, the parcel reference, the payload, the time in Kigali, and the hash of the block before it.

The administrator dashboard checks that every block still agrees with the one before it. A parcel page lists the blocks for that land. Blocks are not edited or deleted from the application.

Who does what

  • Landowners open their own account, add their land, and download or print the record after the UPI and title are issued.
  • Authorized land officials approve that land. Approval issues the UPI and the title, then seals the parcel.
  • The administrator issues official accounts, reviews the audit log, and prepares management reports. The predefined login stays admin / admin.
  • This copy does not sign in to a MINALOC system. Records stay in this application.

Rules on the register

  • A name cannot contain numbers or special characters.
  • A username cannot contain spaces, and it cannot start with a number or a special character.
  • Email addresses are checked, and phone numbers must be Rwandan mobile numbers.
  • A new password must not be less than 6 characters. The preloaded administrator signs in with the issued password.

What this prototype is

It is a private hash chain inside this application, not a public cryptocurrency and not a substitute for a notarised title deed. MINALOC appears as the authority in the case study, and this copy does not sign in to any MINALOC system.
